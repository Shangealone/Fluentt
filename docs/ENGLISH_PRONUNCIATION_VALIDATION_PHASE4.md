## 2. What the two references actually mean

**Dataset side:** `resource/scores.json` words' `phones` from the original SpeechOcean762 source, including ARPABET stress digits. The [publisher's paper, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.01378) says experts first selected among possible canonical sequences after considering the spoken word and voted on a final sequence before assigning phone scores. This is a **human-selected reference tied to that recording**, not necessarily a fixed prompt dictionary. The provided word/phone labels do not include human phone boundaries.

**Model side:** saved `raw_prediction.words[].phonemes[].phone` from actual Phase 2 inference, generated by local `g2p-en` 2.1.0 using installed CMUdict 0.7a/its fallback, with stress digits removed by `pipeline.py`; ARPABET is then mapped to the alignment vocabulary's IPA symbols through `ARPABET_TO_IPA`. The model's displayed phone strings remain stressless ARPABET; the CTC token mapping is a later, separate representation. Installed dictionary SHA-256: `cad209c39eb87677d64e93d97f8eed10b7e6f9bdd42de8e7ca8efc8e17d62e8a`. `pipeline.py`'s map was previously verified identical to the pinned training source. It accepts several IPA **symbol aliases** for one ARPABET phone; those aliases are not a reviewed list of full-word acceptable pronunciations. [CMUdict documentation](https://github.com/cmusphinx/cmudict) records alternate lexical entries and stress markers, and explicitly acknowledges dictionary errors/omissions.

After the same stress-strip rule, **36/37 model sequences** match at least one installed CMUdict entry; `LAYLA` is absent from this dictionary and is likely produced by G2P fallback, but the exact fallback path was not instrumented. **11/37 dataset sequences** match a CMUdict entry. This is evidence about *lexicon coverage*, not which pronunciation was spoken or acceptable. No different alphabet was conflated: both compared strings are stress-stripped ARPABET. **Zero differences are stress-only by construction**; stress correctness is not evaluated by this scorer. A phone-symbol difference may reflect a phonological variant, annotation convention, transcription choice, contextual reduction or reference error. Listening and expert review are required to decide.

Classification is mutually exclusive by the deterministic string rules in `phase4_references.py`. These names describe reference **shapes**, not a validated diagnosis or permission to add a variant. Every row's acceptance decision is **UNRESOLVED**.

| Single vowel-symbol replacement | **21** | `WAS`/`AND` repeat across clips; `BOBBY`, `LAYLA`, `DONKEY`, `NOT`, `TEDDY`, `DO`, `STOPPED`, etc. These may involve vowel choice/reduction; spelling or dictionary membership alone cannot settle them. |

Those are reference comparisons, **not observed learner-phone transcriptions or errors**. ## 4. Proposed policy and human target

The full [variant policy](english-pronunciation-phase4/pronunciation_variant_policy.md) specifies versioned prompt/dictionary provenance, reviewed lexical alternatives, rhotic/non-rhotic and Philippine English considerations, unknown words, alignment failure and an explicit **unable-to-assess** state. Current WavLM runs a **single G2P reference path**; it does not score multiple pronunciations or assess stress. The policy is **documentation only**. No variant is accepted automatically from dictionary membership or from one learner's result. A qualified phonetics reviewer must decide the 37 cases and the planned register before any future implementation.

The full [draft 0–10 rubric](english-pronunciation-phase4/english_pronunciation_rubric_draft.md) targets audible **segmental accuracy** in short fixed readings: 0–2 few recognizable segments despite usable speech; 3–4 frequent serious differences; 5–6 generally intelligible but several clear differences; 7–8 mostly recognizable with a few errors; 9–10 nearly/all accurate. It separately codes prompt mismatch, silence, corruption, noise, clipping and incomplete speech; approved accents do not by themselves reduce a rating. It handles substitutions, omissions, insertions, uncertain phones, variants, self-correction and coverage. Intelligibility is a **separate 0–4 dimension** ideally rated without the visible prompt, while completeness/stress/prosody stay separate. The WavLM output is a mean of learned phone predictions, not a validated 0–10 version of this rubric. The rubric is **DRAFT pending expert and adviser review**; no new human labels were collected.

## 5. Validation and Phase 5 readiness

The [validation protocol](english-pronunciation-phase4/pronunciation_validation_protocol.md) proposes approximately 12 consenting adults × ten fixed prompts and two independent qualified raters. That sample is a feasibility target, **not** a universal power rule. Raters remain blind to WavLM scores; original ratings, quality/coverage decisions, expert adjudication and inter-rater agreement are reported. Pearson/Spearman with speaker-cluster uncertainty are the planned primary association metrics; MAE/RMSE only follow an independently justified common scale. Suggested `r ≥ .60` or MAE `≤ 1.5` remain **PROVISIONAL** and need adviser/rubric/consequence justification. Local consent, retention, restricted access and research approval must precede participant collection. No efficacy for Filipino learners is established by this public dataset.

The [Phase 5 plan](english-pronunciation-phase4/phase5_evaluation_plan.md) used only original test-split identity metadata: 115 remaining eligible speakers after the original ten. Seed **20261101** reserves 30 new development speakers/90 recordings and **30 different untouched evaluation speakers/90 recordings**; exact IDs are in ignored `phase5_cohort_plan.json`. The cohort-selection function did not analyze future cohort ratings, model predictions or audio. The all-sample `scores.json` file was read solely to verify the **old** 30 annotations. The plan is **CONDITIONAL GO for preparation**, not permission to infer: freeze the approved reference/rubric and analysis; verify source hashes/rights and 5-GiB available-RAM gate; disclose or resolve checkpoint training overlap; separate any development-only change from sealed evaluation. The prior 30 are contaminated for claims that Phase 3/4-inspired tuning improved performance. Speaker disjointness within SpeechOcean does **not** prove disjointness from the scorer's private training data. Independence remains **UNVERIFIED**.
