## Draft 1 — Talking about ability

Source reference: [VID-01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hZx7JrIm9lg)

Suggested audience: Grades 7–10, ages 12–16, A2–B1.

Suggested lesson title: **Can, Could, and Be Able To**

Learning goals:

- Choose `can` for present or general ability.
- Choose `could` for general ability in the past.
- Use `be able to` when the sentence needs another tense or a specific ability expression.
- Build affirmative and negative sentences without treating the lesson as a score of language ability.

Draft activities:

1. Multiple choice — **Choose the best verb phrase.**
   - Present ability: “Mia ___ speak two languages.” → `can`
   - “When I was younger, I ___ run very far.” → `could`
   - “Next year, I will ___ join the school team.” → `be able to`
   - A completed success: “After several tries, Leo finally ___ solve the puzzle yesterday.” → `was able to`
2. Word arrangement — **Build the sentence.**
   - `I / can / help / you.`
   - `We / could / see / the mountains.`
   - `She / will / be able to / try again.`
3. Flashcards — `can`, `could`, `be able to`, `was/were able to`, with short original explanations and one original example each.

Suggested metadata: `medium` for the first checks; `hard` for the specific-past-success distinction.

## Draft 2 — Has, have, and had

Source reference: [VID-02](https://youtu.be/38Rvwo8srFE)

Suggested audience: Grades 5–9, ages 10–15, A1–A2.

Suggested lesson title: **Has, Have, and Had**

Learning goals:

- Match `has` with `he`, `she`, `it`, or a singular noun subject in the present; `I` and `you` use `have`.
- Match `have` with `I`, `you`, `we`, `they`, and plural subjects in the present.
- Use `had` for possession in the past with any subject.
- Form simple negative possession sentences with `doesn't have`, `don't have`, and `didn't have`.

Draft activities:

1. Multiple choice — **Choose the correct verb.**
   - “The teacher ___ a blue notebook.” → `has`
   - “My friends ___ new bags.” → `have`
   - “We ___ a small garden last year.” → `had`
   - Make this negative: “Nico ___ a pencil today.” → `doesn't have`
   - Make this negative: “They ___ a car when they were younger.” → `didn't have`
2. Word arrangement — **Build the sentence.**
   - `She / has / a / red / umbrella.`
   - `We / have / three / science / books.`
   - `I / didn't have / a / bicycle / then.`
3. Flashcards — `has`, `have`, `had`, `doesn't have`, `don't have`, and `didn't have`, each with a short subject-and-time reminder.

Suggested metadata: `easy` for present-tense selection; `medium` for negative and past-tense sentences.

## Draft 3 — Direct and polite intentions

Source reference: [VID-03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iZYbil-RV9Q)

Suggested audience: Grades 7–12, ages 12–17, A2–B1.

Suggested lesson title: **A Friendly Way to Ask**

Learning goals:

- Recognize a direct present intention with `I just want to…`.
- Recognize the softer, more polite tone of `I just wanted to…` in a present conversation.
- Choose wording that fits a friend, teacher, stranger, or school office.
- Build short requests without saying that one form is always “wrong.”

Draft activities:

1. Multiple choice — **Choose the expression for the requested tone.** Both forms can be appropriate with friends or teachers; assess the stated tone, not the person's role.
   - Use a direct present intention: “I just ___ to ask about the game.” → `want`
   - Use a softer introduction to a present request: “I just ___ to check my schedule.” → `wanted`
   - Use a softer introduction in a message: “I just ___ to ask if the meeting is still today.” → `wanted`
   - Use a direct present intention: “I just ___ to borrow your ruler.” → `want`
2. Word arrangement — **Build the request.**
   - `I / just / want / to / ask / something.`
   - `I / just / wanted / to / check / the time.`
   - `I / just / wanted / to / say / thank you.`
3. Flashcards — `direct`, `polite`, `intention`, `I just want to…`, and `I just wanted to…`, with context-based explanations.

Suggested metadata: `medium`.

## Flashcard backs

| Lesson | Front | Original back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ability | can | Present or general ability. Use can + base verb: I can draw a bird. Negative: I cannot draw a map. |
| Ability | could | General ability in the past: At six, I could ride a bike. Use the base verb after could. |
| Ability | be able to | Express ability when another form is needed: After more practice, I will be able to swim. |
| Ability | was/were able to | Ability in the past; often used for a specific completed success: We were able to finish the project yesterday. |
| Possession | has | Present possession with he, she, it, or a singular noun: My brother has a kite. |
| Possession | have | Present possession with I, you, we, they, or plural nouns: You have a colorful bag. |
| Possession | had | Past possession with any subject: Our class had a pet fish last year. |
| Possession | doesn't have | Present negative with he, she, it, or a singular noun: Sam doesn't have a ruler. |
| Possession | don't have | Present negative with I, you, we, they, or plural nouns: We don't have any chalk. |
| Possession | didn't have | Past negative with any subject: I didn't have my lunchbox yesterday. |
| Tone | direct | States an intention clearly: I just want to ask a question. Direct wording can still be friendly. |
| Tone | polite | Shows consideration through wording, tone, and context: Excuse me. I just wanted to ask a question. |
| Tone | intention | Something you plan or wish to do: I want to learn a new song. |
| Tone | I just want to… | Introduces a direct present intention: I just want to check our homework. |
| Tone | I just wanted to… | Can introduce a softer present request or describe a past intention. Use the surrounding conversation to decide: I just wanted to ask about tomorrow's trip. |
